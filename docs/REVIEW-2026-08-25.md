# Comprehensive review

**Doc version:** 1.0 · **Updated:** 2026-08-25 · **Applies to:** app v0.3.2

Four lenses: systems architecture, performance engineering, UI/UX, and project practice.
Findings are evidence-based with `file:line` references; the five most consequential were
verified by hand before publication.

---

## Verdict

This is a **well-architected project with a systematic verification gap**. The design decisions
are unusually good - better than most commercial software of this size. The problem is that
several of them are **documented but not wired up**, and a 938-test suite did not notice,
because the tests examine functions rather than the assembled system.

Nothing here is a design failure. Everything here is an integration failure.

## The pattern worth understanding first

Five independent findings share one shape. In each case a decision was made, documented
carefully, implemented - and then not connected.

| Documented | Reality |
|---|---|
| Search cache with generation-based invalidation | `SearchEngine` is constructed at `main.py:146`, `cli.py:994`, `cli.py:1623` - **`cache=` is passed at none of them**. `self.cache` is always `None`; the entire `_cache_key` apparatus (`engine.py:348-402`) is dead code. **There is no warm search.** The <300ms p95 target is stated against a tier that does not exist |
| "Both retrievers run in parallel" (`engine.py:263`) | Both hit one shared `sqlite3.Connection` (`sqlite_store.py:141-146`). The connection mutex serialises them |
| Embedding batch of 256, "the single biggest throughput lever" | `EMBED_BATCH = 256` (`pipeline.py:97`) is silently re-split into four calls of `EMBED_BATCH = 64` (`embedder.py:44`). Two different constants, same name |
| `SearchView.shutdown()` prevents a teardown race | It stops `_typing_timer`, `_idle_timer`, `_timer` (`search_view.py:166-174`). The timers are called `_interim_timer` and `_full_timer` (`:137,:142`). `getattr(..., None)` means it fails **silently**. The documented race is live on every close |
| "Rerank results" toggle in Settings | `rerank_toggled` is emitted (`settings_view.py:88`) and connected nowhere. Two controls named the same thing, in two places, neither working |

**The documentation became a substitute for verification.** It is good enough to be believed,
including by the people writing it.

There is already precedent in the project's own record. `HANDOFF.md` §3b notes that the first
time search itself was measured - rather than its parts - nineteen of twenty queries returned
zero results, a fault a thousand passing tests had missed. That lesson was learned locally and
not generalised. This review is the same lesson, at system scale.

## Lens 1: systems architecture

### What is right, and worth protecting

The single-process decision is correct and well defended. Embedded SQLite + LanceDB + ONNX with
no services is the right shape for a single-user desktop tool, and the reasoning in
`HANDOFF.md` §3a for removing the knowledge graph and cancelling two layers is better product
thinking than most teams manage.

**"SQLite is the authority; LanceDB is derived"** is the strongest decision in the codebase. It
makes every failure recoverable and every corruption fixable by rebuild. Protect it.

The `AppError` contract - code, message, suggestion, action type - is genuinely excellent, and
the `SKIP_CONTINUE` semantics are exactly right for a 100GB run.

### Structural problems

**A1 - CRITICAL: one connection defeats WAL.** `check_same_thread=False` with a lock on writes
only (`sqlite_store.py:215`). Search-pool threads (`engine.py:175`), a `SearchWorker` per
keystroke (`ui/workers.py:187`) and the indexer all share it. WAL's many-readers/one-writer
property - the entire reason WAL was chosen - is nullified. A read issued during
`BEGIN IMMEDIATE` blocks. **Fix: a connection per thread**, or a small read pool plus the
existing single writer.

**A2 - HIGH: the search layer owns the storage layer's SQL.** `keyword.py:171,201,216` and
`vector.py:174` reach into `store.conn` and write raw queries against the schema. The
consequence is real: the indexes `schema.sql` must provide are determined by a module that
cannot see it, which is exactly how findings P2 and P3 below came to exist unnoticed.

**A3 - MEDIUM: `SqliteStore` is a god object.** 1,281 lines spanning files, chunks, mail, FTS,
usage analytics **and a deprecated knowledge graph** (`:822-1196`) including
`merge_contained_entities`, an O(n²)-per-bucket routine issuing SQL per pair (`:1087`). The
graph was removed from the product; its store methods were not.

**A4 - MEDIUM: filter logic exists three times.** `keyword._filter_sql` (`:38`),
`browse_messages` (`:348`), `search_files_by_name` (`:465`) - hand-built LIKE/IN predicates
with **different escaping rules**; only `browse_messages` escapes `%` and `_`. A user searching
for a literal `%` gets different behaviour in three places.

**A5 - `app/cli.py` is 1,864 lines**, the largest module in the project, now carrying
subcommands for every layer.

## Lens 2: performance engineering

The stated target is p95 under 300ms at 20-30M chunks. **The current design cannot reach it**,
and the reasons are structural rather than incidental.

**P1 - CRITICAL: no cache exists.** See the table above. Every query pays full cost, always.

**P2 - CRITICAL: multi-word queries OR across the corpus.** `AND_TERM_LIMIT = 1`
(`query.py:510`) means any query with two or more content terms is joined with `OR`
(`:570-573`). FTS5 must union every posting list and score every matching row before
`ORDER BY score LIMIT 100` (`keyword.py:166`). Free at 10k chunks; at 20M a three-word
natural-language query - **the primary use case** - touches millions of rows per keystroke.

This value was tuned against the 21-document fixture corpus (`query.py:495-500`). It is the
single largest p95 risk in the project, and it is a one-line change made for good reasons at a
scale that no longer applies.

**P3 - CRITICAL: an unbounded id set on the critical path, serially.**
`file_ids_matching` runs `SELECT id FROM files WHERE ...` → `fetchall()` → `set`
(`keyword.py:216-219`) **before** the two futures are submitted (`engine.py:266-270`) - so it
is serial, not parallel. `has_filters` is true whenever `scope != "all"` (`query.py:167`), so
**clicking the Mail chip full-scans `files`**. There is no index on `files.source_kind`, despite
a comment at `keyword.py:83` asserting there is.

**P4 - CRITICAL: LanceDB will degrade continuously and never recovers.** A `delete` plus an
`add` per document (`pipeline.py:800`), including on first index when there is nothing to
delete; `count_rows()` on every batch (`vector_store.py:219`). At 20M chunks that is millions of
dataset versions and ~78k append commits, and **no `optimize` or `compact_files` call exists
anywhere in the codebase**. This is the hardest scalability cliff here - the index gets slower
every run, permanently.

**P5 - HIGH: unindexable predicates.** `LOWER(f.path) LIKE '%x%'` (`keyword.py:59`),
`LOWER(REPLACE(f.path, f.parent_dir, '')) LIKE '%x%'` (`:70`), and
`LOWER(sender|recipients|subject) LIKE '%x%'` (`:100-118`). Functions on columns plus leading
wildcards mean a full scan per predicate; `idx_messages_sender` can never be used.

**P6 - HIGH: `_filter_only` scans everything.** `WHERE c.ordinal = 0 ORDER BY f.mtime_ns DESC`
(`keyword.py:190-200`) - `idx_chunks_file_ord` is `(file_id, ordinal)`, so `ordinal` is not
leading. A 20M-row scan plus a sort. No index on `files.mtime_ns` either, which `after:` and
`before:` also need.

**P7 - HIGH: ~4 commits per document, ~80M transactions at scale.** `upsert_file`,
`replace_chunks`, `set_message`, `mark_indexed` each take their own `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, and
`mark_indexed` runs in a per-file loop (`pipeline.py:862`). `upsert_file` also issues a
redundant `SELECT id` after its own upsert (`:276`).

**P8 - HIGH: rerank is inline, synchronous and on by default.** `want_rerank` defaults to
`reranker.available` (`engine.py:341`), and 30 cross-encoder passages run on the critical path
(`:290`). Its own docstring budgets 200ms - two-thirds of the entire target, spent by default.

**P9 - MEDIUM: pure-Python float loops per vector.** `list(map(float, vector))`
(`embedder.py:163`), `math.sqrt(sum(v*v for v in vector))` (`:210`), then
`[float(x) for x in vectors[i]]` again (`vector_store.py:209`). Roughly 3 × 384 interpreter-level
operations × 20M chunks, on the thread holding the GIL - which also blocks searches. NumPy is
already a transitive dependency.

**P10 - MEDIUM: quadratic chunker.** `any(start >= position > previous_end for position in
paragraph_starts)` (`chunker.py:141`) - O(words × paragraphs) per document.

**P11 - MEDIUM: whole-file reads with a 2GB ceiling.** `path.read_bytes()`
(`plaintext.py:102`), `max_file_bytes = 2GB` (`walker.py:145`), then a full decode and a
line-list rebuild (`base.py:141-154`): 4-5× file size resident, multiplied by extraction
workers.

**P12 - MEDIUM: `_bump_generation` fires on every write** (`sqlite_store.py:287,574,1237`).
Once a cache is connected, any incremental index run invalidates it continuously, making it
useless exactly when it matters.

**P13 - MEDIUM: two write transactions per search for usage logging** (`engine.py:439-446`),
contending with the indexer for the write lock. `search_hits` grows unbounded with no retention
policy and no `chunk_id` index.

**P14 - MEDIUM: no tuning pragmas, no `ANALYZE`, no FTS5 `optimize`.** Default 2MB page cache
against a multi-gigabyte index; `PRAGMA optimize` only at `close()` (`:167`). FTS5 segments
accumulate across every incremental run.

## Lens 3: UI and UX

**U1 - CRITICAL: the shutdown race is live.** `search_view.py:166-174`, above. `closeEvent`
calls it and then pumps events for four seconds (`shell.py:866`), giving the still-running
timers ample opportunity to start a search against a closing store.

**U2 - CRITICAL: blocking I/O on the UI thread, on the hot path.** This directly contradicts
the standing rule just adopted:

- `results_view.py:151` calls `Path(path).exists()` **per row on every rebuild** - and rebuild
  runs on every result, expand, collapse and preference change. Fifty `stat()` calls per
  debounce; on a disconnected network root, seconds each.
- `search_view.py:270` runs a SQLite query inside `_on_results`.
- `search_view.py:294` performs a **database write inside the click handler**, before the file
  opens.
- Also `files_view.py:186,304`, `mail_view.py:250`, `settings_view.py:222,257`.

**U3 - HIGH: results can be unreadable.** `result_delegate.py:111-114` uses
`option.palette.text()`, but theming is by stylesheet and `theme.py:88` covers
`QListWidget`/`QTableWidget`/`QPlainTextEdit` - **not `QListView`**, which is what results now
use. Forcing dark mode on a light-mode Windows paints near-black text on `#1e1f22`.

**U4 - HIGH: mail sorts wrongly.** `mail_view.py:227` stores real values in `UserRole+1`, but
`QTableWidgetItem.__lt__` compares `DisplayRole`. "10 KB" sorts before "3 KB"; dates sort
alphabetically - the precise failure the comment claims to prevent.

**U5 - HIGH: the View menu silently resets preferences.** `view_options.py:317,357` construct
`ViewPreferences(prefs.columns, ...)` positionally, dropping `group_by_document` and
`show_scores`. **Changing the text size turns grouping back on.**

**U6 - HIGH: dead controls.** `rerank_toggled` and `cloud_toggled` connected nowhere;
"Index cloud-only files" read live but never persisted, so it silently resets each launch;
`mail_view.opened` never connected, so double-click on a message does nothing and Mail has no
Enter handler at all - **mail is unreachable by keyboard**; `ui:tray_minimise`/`ui:tray_close`
read but never written; `set_next_run` never called, so "Next run" is permanently blank.

**U7 - MEDIUM: the best empty-state message is overwritten one line later.**
`search_view.py:278` sets it; `:281` unconditionally replaces it with `status`.

**U8 - MEDIUM: widget churn during indexing.** `indexing_view.py:259` rebuilds a `_SkipRow`
(3-4 labels and a button) per skip code on **every progress tick**, for the hours a 100GB index
takes.

**U9 - MEDIUM: `processEvents` inside `closeEvent`** (`shell.py:908`) - the exact call the new
standing rule forbids, re-entering the event loop while views are being destroyed.

**U10 - MEDIUM: Interpret searches twice.** `search_view.py:333` `setText` fires `textChanged`,
restarting both timers; `:336` then dispatches immediately. A second full pipeline run lands
400ms later.

**U11 - MEDIUM: scroll position carries into a new query** (`results_view.py:128`), landing a
fresh search mid-list with the top hits off-screen.

**Accessibility**: no accessible names anywhere in `app/ui` - notably the unlabelled per-row
checkbox at `file_types.py:121`; hardcoded pixel font sizes (`theme.py:79,84,134...`) override
the OS text-size setting by design; the `warn` state renders identically to normal because
`#statWarn` has no rule; matches are signalled by weight alone because `#resultSnippet b`
(`theme.py:145`) never applies.

## Lens 4: project practice

### Genuinely excellent

The `HANDOFF.md` / `PROJECT_INSTRUCTIONS.md` pair, version-stamped and test-enforced, is better
continuity discipline than most professional teams maintain. §3a - decisions **with what would
reverse them** - is exemplary. Cancelling two layers on evidence was the right call, made well.

The `AppError` contract, the diagnostic bundle with redaction, the structured log split, the
generated fixtures: all strong.

### The gap

**938 tests, 15,655 lines of test code, and not one of them noticed** a dead cache, a dead
toggle, a no-op shutdown, or two constants with the same name and different values.

That is not a shortage of tests. It is a **category** of test that does not exist: nothing
asserts that the assembled application is wired the way the documents say. Every test asks
"does this function return what I expect"; none asks "is this function reachable from the
running program".

**The cheapest high-value work in this project is a small suite of wiring tests**: construct
the real objects the way `main.py` does, and assert the properties the documentation claims -
the cache is present, every declared signal has a receiver, every preference persists across a
round trip, every timer named in `shutdown()` exists.

A test-ratio of 1.6:1 is healthy. The ratio is not the problem. The blind spot is.

## Prioritised remediation

**Before indexing 100GB** - each of these is far cheaper now than after:

1. **P4** LanceDB compaction, and stop deleting on first index. Unrecoverable once the index is
   built.
2. **P2** `AND_TERM_LIMIT` - re-tune against a realistic corpus, not 21 documents.
3. **A1** Connection per thread.
4. **P3, P5, P6** The missing indexes: `files.source_kind`, `files.mtime_ns`, and a strategy for
   the LIKE predicates. Then `ANALYZE`.
5. **P7** Batch the per-document transactions.

**Before the next release:**

6. **U1, U2, U9** The shutdown race and UI-thread I/O - the standing rule is currently violated
   in the file that adopted it.
7. **P1, P12** Wire the cache; stop invalidating it per file.
8. **U3** Theme the `QListView`, or results are unreadable in one valid configuration.
9. **U6** The dead controls. A setting that silently does nothing is worse than no setting.
10. **U4, U5** Sorting and preference clobbering.

**Then:**

11. The wiring-test suite described above.
12. **P8** Move rerank off the critical path, or default it off until measured.
13. **A3, A4** Delete the dead graph store methods; unify the filter SQL.
14. **P9, P10, P11** NumPy in the embed path; fix the quadratic chunker; stream large files.

## Closing

The architecture is sound, the product judgement is better than sound, and the documentation is
exceptional. The gap is between what the documents assert and what the assembled program does -
and the documents are persuasive enough that nobody checked.

The fix is not more tests. It is one new kind of test, and a habit: **when a document claims
the system does something, assert it against the system, not against a function.**
